    Two familiar faces. Plenty of room to practise.

    Meet the anime teaching characters Dr Palwasha and Dr Daniyal, illustrated from the photos supplied for this guide. They will help you listen, explain and hand over. Their conversations here are invented learning examples, not accounts of their real work or medical endorsements. Even the most confident-looking doctor is allowed a second attempt at a very long German word.

    Palwasha · listen for the worry

    The question hiding behind the question

    A patient asks whether an examination will hurt. Dr Palwasha does not answer with a blanket promise. She first asks what worries the patient most. A past painful procedure, a fear of needles and uncertainty about being alone call for different explanations. Listening is how the next sentence becomes useful.

    „Was macht Ihnen bei der Untersuchung am meisten Sorgen?“

    What worries you most about the examination?

    Daniyal · explain one small step

    The machine is large. The explanation can be small.

    A patient says the MRI looks like a spaceship. Dr Daniyal agrees that the machine is unfamiliar, then explains what will happen and invites a question. He checks the person's specific concerns and leaves individual safety screening to the responsible team. The goal is understanding, not winning a competition for the longest scanner description.

    „Ich erkläre Ihnen zuerst den Ablauf. Danach besprechen wir Ihre Fragen und die Sicherheitsabklärung.“

    First I will explain what happens. Then we will discuss your questions and the safety checks.

    Together · keep the facts intact

    The handover is not a game of telephone.

    Dr Palwasha has heard that abdominal pain began yesterday. She has not yet asked exactly where it hurts. Dr Daniyal needs both pieces: the known timeline and the missing location. A beautifully tidy report that fills the blank with a guess is still wrong. They keep the blank visible and make the next question clear.

    „Die Schmerzen bestehen laut Patient seit gestern. Die genaue Lokalisation habe ich noch nicht erfragt.“

    According to the patient, the pain has been present since yesterday. I have not yet asked the precise location.

    Together · make revision human

    One sentence better than yesterday

    The first handover is a little tangled. They stop, laugh at the sentence rather than at each other, and try again. This time Dr Daniyal gives the timeline first. Dr Palwasha checks whether the rest matches what the patient actually said. The plant is an excellent listener, although its written feedback remains disappointing.

    „Lassen Sie mich das noch einmal klarer formulieren.“

    Let me put that more clearly.
