    The supplied source¶

    FSP in Bayern 2024, second edition, Shamil Gurbanov. Supplied as DOC-20260925-WA0007.pdf, 255 physical pages. The file includes clinical-topic examples, a terminology section, empathy phrases, procedure explanations and examination material.

    This is an independent educational guide, not an official edition, endorsement or reproduction of the book. The original PDF and extracted text are not redistributed in the repository. Page references are topic pointers; the new fictional dialogues are not quotations from those pages. Printed page numbers generally equal physical PDF page minus three. Ranges in the table show topic coverage; each individual exercise cites the pages used.

    Current exam information¶

    The Bavarian medical chamber's FSP page describes the current exam structure and requirements. Its procedure rules are a separate official reference. Checked 25 September 2026. We deliberately avoid promising exact exam cases, timings of appointments, or a pass based on this guide.

    How we treat uncertain or unsafe source wording¶

    We do not repeat blanket assurances that a procedure is painless or risk-free, fixed preparation instructions that ignore the individual plan, or certainty about a diagnosis before assessment. We preserve the communication goal and teach appropriate uncertainty. Emergency scenarios stop the routine script and seek the clinical team. Drug doses and treatment algorithms are outside this guide.

    The cases have undergone automated structure checks and editorial review for consistency. They are not independently clinically validated. Qualified German-language and clinical review is recommended before adopting the guide as formal institutional training.

    Additional fact checks¶

    • Listening with care · University Hospitals of Leicester: Pregnancy checks before X-ray — Possible pregnancy informs an individual examination decision; necessary X-ray imaging is not categorically forbidden.
    • Leg circulation · NHS: Peripheral arterial disease — Walking-related leg discomfort and rest symptoms can be relevant to arterial disease; history alone does not confirm a diagnosis.
    • Leg circulation · NHS Highland: Vascular guidelines — Sudden pain, coldness and movement or sensory change may indicate acute limb ischaemia requiring urgent specialist assessment; no treatment algorithm is reproduced.
    • Leg circulation · NHS: Venous leg ulcer diagnosis — Arterial circulation assessment is relevant before selecting compression for a leg ulcer.
    • Blood clots · NHS: Deep vein thrombosis — New leg pain and swelling can warrant urgent assessment for a possible clot.
    • Blood clots · NHS: Pulmonary embolism — Sudden breathlessness and chest pain in a possible clot presentation require urgent assessment; severe symptoms require emergency help.
    • Blood clots · Cambridge University Hospitals: Ultrasound scan of leg veins — Venous ultrasound assesses for clots and helps inform treatment planning; the case avoids an absolute comfort or result promise.
    • Wounds · NHS: Venous leg ulcer diagnosis — Assessment of arterial blood supply is relevant before choosing compression.
    • Wounds · NHS: Venous leg ulcer treatment — Wound and compression care need appropriately trained clinical staff; the cases do not teach a dressing or bandaging protocol.
    • Wounds · NHS: Sudden confusion — New confusion warrants urgent assessment rather than being treated as routine follow-up.
    • Chest pain · NHS: Heart attack — Ongoing chest pressure with radiation or associated concerning symptoms can require emergency assessment. The cases teach escalation rather than drug treatment.
    • Chest pain · NHS: Electrocardiogram — An ECG records cardiac electrical activity; explaining that purpose is distinct from interpreting its result.
    • Heart failure · NHS: Heart failure — Breathlessness during activity or lying down, swelling and weight changes can be relevant. Heart failure does not mean the heart has stopped. These are prompts for assessment, not diagnostic rules.
    • Heart failure · NHS England: Managing heart failure at home — Individual care planning and communication about changing symptoms matter; the guide does not prescribe a universal fluid limit or medication schedule.
    • Cough and breathing · NHS: Pneumonia — Respiratory illness may involve cough, fever and breathlessness; severe breathing difficulty requires urgent help.
    • Cough and breathing · NHS: Tonsillitis — Inability to swallow and breathing difficulty during a severe throat illness are warning symptoms requiring emergency assessment.
    • Cough and breathing · NICE NG120: Acute cough evidence summary — Green sputum alone did not identify an acute-cough subgroup more likely to benefit from antibiotics in the cited evidence; the case therefore avoids colour-only prescribing.
    • Asthma · NHS: Asthma — Asthma symptoms can vary and worsen; severe or worsening attacks need urgent help. Regimens differ, so the guide does not teach one colour-based or universal dosing plan.
    • Asthma · NHS: Spirometry — Spirometry involves guided breathing measurements used in lung-function assessment.
    • Swallowing · NHS: Symptoms of oesophageal cancer — Swallowing difficulty warrants assessment; symptoms alone do not confirm cancer.
    • Swallowing · NHS: Sore throat emergency advice — Inability to swallow or drooling is a reason for emergency assessment, not a routine interview or a trial drink.
    • Stomach pain · NHS: Stomach ulcer — Coffee-ground or bloody vomit can signal bleeding requiring urgent assessment; upper abdominal symptoms have more than one possible cause.
    • Gallbladder · NHS: Gallstones — ERCP targets bile duct stones; gallbladder removal is a different procedure. Pain with jaundice or shivering requires urgent assessment. An incidental gallstone does not automatically require treatment.
    • Bowel inflammation · NHS: Inflammatory bowel disease — Persistent bowel symptoms and bleeding warrant assessment; severe symptoms may need urgent help. Communication cases do not infer a diagnosis from symptom duration alone.
    • Bowel cancer · NHS: Symptoms of bowel cancer — Changed bowel habit and bleeding warrant assessment but do not alone establish cancer; ongoing heavy rectal bleeding requires emergency help.
    • Thyroid · NHS: Underactive thyroid — Fatigue, cold sensitivity and concentration difficulty can occur with underactive thyroid but need individual assessment.
    • Thyroid · NHS: Overactive thyroid — Palpitations, shaking and heat sensitivity may accompany overactive thyroid; symptoms require assessment rather than an assumption of anxiety. Treatment options need individual specialist discussion.
    • Blood sugar · NHS: Low blood sugar — Possible low glucose with impaired responsiveness needs prompt help. Do not give oral food or drink to an unconscious person. Current symptoms require current assessment rather than reassurance from a prior long-term marker.
    • Blood and lymph nodes · NHS: Symptoms of Hodgkin lymphoma — Painless swelling and night sweats merit assessment but do not establish a lymphoma diagnosis.
    • Blood and lymph nodes · The Christie NHS: Acute myeloid leukaemia — Unexplained bruising and gum bleeding can be features of blood disease and deserve evaluation; the cases do not diagnose leukaemia from symptoms alone.
    • Headache · NHS: Migraine — Sudden extremely severe headache or new acute neurological symptoms require emergency assessment. Light sensitivity can accompany migraine but does not independently confirm it. Headache and medicine-use patterns are relevant to review.
    • Seizures · NHS: Epilepsy — A seizure does not automatically establish epilepsy. Repeated events without recovery need emergency help. Pregnancy planning needs specialist medication review; patients should not make independent treatment changes. Driving decisions need individual advice and applicable local requirements, not a generic clearance date.
    • Seizures · NICE NG217: Diagnosis and assessment of epilepsy — A normal EEG must not be used by itself to exclude epilepsy.
    • Throat symptoms · NHS: Tonsillitis — Breathing difficulty with throat symptoms warrants urgent help; treatment should not be assumed from the symptom label alone.
    • Wrist injuries · NHS: Broken arm or wrist — Injury symptoms need assessment; new sensory complaints should be brought promptly to clinical attention.
    • Wrist injuries · Kingston and Richmond NHS: After a wrist fracture — Finger movement can coexist with a wrist fracture; it is not a stand-alone exclusion test.
    • Major injuries · NHS: Spleen problems — Symptoms after abdominal injury may appear or worsen later; concerning symptoms require urgent assessment.
    • Spine and back pain · Somerset NHS: Cauda equina syndrome — New bladder changes or altered saddle sensation with back symptoms require emergency assessment; the scenarios do not supply a diagnosis or treatment protocol.
    • Kidneys and urine · NHS: Kidney infection — New confusion during an infection-related illness is a reason for emergency help.
    • Imaging tests · NHS: MRI scan — MRI preparation includes individual screening for implanted devices and anxiety; specialist interpretation follows acquisition.
    • Imaging tests · NHS: CT scan — CT preparation depends on the examination; prior contrast reactions and pregnancy possibility should be discussed with the team.
    • Imaging tests · Cambridge University Hospitals: Nuclear medicine — Nuclear medicine uses radioactive tracers and provides examination-specific information.
    • Imaging tests · Cambridge University Hospitals: PET-CT — Radiation-protection advice may include temporary close-contact precautions; this supports asking the specific service rather than inventing one interval for all tracer studies.
    • Medical procedures · NHS: ECG — ECG records cardiac electrical activity.
    • Medical procedures · NHS: Echocardiogram — A transthoracic echo uses a probe on the chest to examine the heart.
    • Medical procedures · NHS: Gastroscopy — Sedation changes aftercare and transport planning; follow the service instructions.
    • Medical procedures · NHS: Colonoscopy — Bowel preparation belongs to the specific endoscopy plan; sedation is not equivalent to an automatic full anaesthetic.
    • Medical procedures · NHS: EEG — EEG records electrical brain activity and requires professional interpretation.
    • Medical procedures · NHS: Kidney stone treatment — Shock-wave treatment may cause discomfort and may require more than one session; no universal success promise is appropriate.
    • Medicines · NHS: Anticoagulants — Anticoagulants have bleeding considerations; procedure and dosing questions require individual professional advice.
    • Medicines · NHS: Herbal medicines — Herbal products should be included when checking potential medicine interactions.
    • Physical examination · NHS: Consent to treatment — Agreement follows understandable information; a signature alone does not demonstrate understanding.
