    The examination with a pause button

    Leonie is told that a rectal examination may be part of assessing her symptoms. She goes quiet and folds the information leaflet into a precise square. She is not refusing, but she has not agreed either. A busy colleague mistakes the silence for permission and reaches for supplies. The learning task is to stop that assumption, explain the purpose and process in respectful language, offer appropriate support and make clear that Leonie can ask questions or ask for the examination to stop.

    Leonie Weber, 54

    Leonie is told that a rectal examination may be part of assessing her symptoms. Dr Daniyal appears as a fictional teaching character.

    Patient

    Was genau soll dabei passieren?

    English meaning

    What exactly is supposed to happen?

    Dr Daniyal

    Ich erkläre zuerst den Zweck und den Ablauf. Danach entscheiden Sie.

    English meaning

    I will first explain the purpose and the process. Then you decide.

    Patient

    Kann jemand dabei sein, und kann ich Stopp sagen?

    English meaning

    Can someone be present, and can I say stop?

    Dr Daniyal

    Ja, wir besprechen eine Begleitperson und unterbrechen, wenn Sie das möchten.

    English meaning

    Yes, we will discuss a chaperone and stop if you want us to.

    Practice misstep

    The conversation gets stuck

    Practice misstep: Treat silence as agreement.

    Better approach

    Make room for understanding

    Pause before any examination. Explain what is proposed, why, and what the person may experience, then invite questions and check consent. Offer an appropriate chaperone according to local practice and protect privacy. Do not interpret silence as agreement, promise no discomfort or proceed simply because the examination appears on a routine checklist.

    What changed—and why it helps¶

    An intimate examination requires more than logistical readiness. Silence can mean uncertainty, fear or a wish to ask something without knowing how. A clear explanation and an opportunity to decide make participation possible. Consent is an ongoing conversation, so a previous yes does not remove the person’s ability to ask for a pause.

    Your turn

    Read the explanation

    Best next move: Explain, offer support and obtain consent before proceeding

    The right move respects Leonie’s control and provides information before action. Silence does not establish consent, and calling an examination routine does not remove the need to explain it. Keep the language direct and respectful, offer appropriate support and make the option to stop clear throughout the process.
