    The first floor used to be the warm-up

    Monika lives on the fourth floor. She says she is “a little slower,” which sounds mild until she explains that she now stops on the first landing to catch her breath. Last month she could reach her door without stopping. She jokes that the stairs have added extra steps when nobody was watching. The learner needs her previous and current ability, the timescale and any symptoms at rest. The building’s floor number alone does not explain what changed, and a social-history box will not capture the functional loss.

    Meet your patient
    Monika Hess, 61
    Practise

    Pull up a chair. Listen first.

    A history focuses on declining exercise tolerance. Dr Palwasha appears as a fictional teaching character.

    Patient

    Schon im ersten Stock muss ich Luft holen.

    English meaning

    I already have to catch my breath on the first floor.

    Dr Palwasha

    Wie war das vor einem Monat?

    English meaning

    What was it like a month ago?

    Patient

    Da ging es bis nach oben ohne Pause.

    English meaning

    Then I could get all the way up without a break.

    Dr Palwasha

    Ich halte diese Veränderung fest und frage auch nach Beschwerden in Ruhe.

    English meaning

    I will record that change and also ask about symptoms at rest.

    Practice misstep

    The conversation gets stuck

    Practice misstep: Fourth-floor resident: exercise capacity adequate.

    Better approach

    Make room for understanding

    Ask what Monika could do before and what she can do now, using the same activity for comparison. Clarify the pace of change and associated symptoms, including current rest symptoms. Arrange assessment based on the overall presentation. Keep the actual change in the handover instead of replacing it with a vague label such as reduced fitness.

    See the communication skill

    The stairs are part of the history

    The patient’s own baseline is often more informative than a general expectation for age or housing. Comparing the same staircase over time makes the change visible. It also avoids assuming that someone who still reaches home can do so comfortably or without a clinically relevant loss of function.

    Your turn

    Which comparison is most informative?

    Read the explanation

    Best next move: The same stairs now versus last month

    The same activity at two points in time shows a meaningful functional change. Comparing neighbours introduces different bodies and habits, while shoe size does not answer the question. The learner should preserve Monika’s report and arrange assessment rather than infer a cause from the comparison alone.
