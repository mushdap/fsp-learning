    A new colour changes the conversation

    Hanna was discussing intermittent abdominal pain when she adds that her eyes have looked yellow since yesterday and she is now shivering. She has not had a current clinical assessment. Her husband wants to read out the previous ultrasound report first, but the new symptoms matter more than completing that document review. This scene contains no comic detour. Your task is to identify the change clearly, stop routine questioning and summon the clinical team promptly rather than assume that a known gallstone explains everything safely.

    Meet your patient
    Hanna Lorenz, 65
    Practise
    Escalate pain with reported jaundice and shivering
    Scroll to begin ↓
    German words · human meaning

    Pull up a chair. Listen first.

    Hanna was discussing intermittent abdominal pain when she adds that her eyes have looked yellow since yesterday and she is now shivering. Dr Palwasha appears as a fictional teaching character.

    Patient

    Seit gestern sind meine Augen gelb, und jetzt friere ich stark.

    English meaning

    My eyes have been yellow since yesterday, and now I am shivering badly.

    Dr Palwasha

    Diese Veränderung muss sofort ärztlich beurteilt werden.

    English meaning

    This change needs immediate clinical assessment.

    Patient

    Soll mein Mann erst den alten Bericht vorlesen?

    English meaning

    Should my husband read the old report first?

    Dr Palwasha

    Nein, ich hole jetzt das Team. Den Bericht nehmen wir dazu.

    English meaning

    No, I am getting the team now. We will include the report.

    Practice misstep

    The conversation gets stuck

    Practice misstep: Read the entire previous report before escalating.

    Better approach

    Make room for understanding

    Interrupt the routine review and obtain urgent clinical assessment. Report abdominal pain with newly described yellow eyes and shivering, distinguishing the patient’s account from observations not yet made. Bring the previous report without letting its review delay help. Avoid naming a confirmed obstruction or infection before the team evaluates her.

    What changed—and why it helps¶

    An old diagnosis does not make every new symptom expected or harmless. The changed combination raises concern and changes the priority of the conversation. A concise escalation gives the team the essential story. It is possible to act urgently while remaining honest that the exact cause and current measurements are unknown.

    A small decision

    Your turn

    Hanna now reports yellow eyes and shivering with abdominal pain. What should happen first?

    Read the explanation

    Best next move: Summon the clinical team for the new symptoms now

    The changed symptoms require prompt assessment. Reading the whole report first delays that response, while calling jaundice normal risks false reassurance. State what Hanna reports and what remains unmeasured. The old information may help the assessment, but it should accompany urgent action rather than become a condition for starting it.

    A language-practice handover

    Same person. A colleague needs the key details.
