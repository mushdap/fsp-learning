    Burning is not the same as going often

    Timo says he has trouble with water, then looks relieved when you ask what he means. He is not sure whether to describe frequency, burning or the amount. At home he can explain a complicated coffee machine to anyone, but the clinic vocabulary makes a simple bodily function sound like engineering. You need short, separate questions rather than one sentence containing five symptoms. The goal is to discover his actual experience, including what has not yet been answered, without steering him into agreeing with everything.

    Meet your patient
    Timo Rauch, 44
    Practise

    German words · human meaning

    Pull up a chair. Listen first.

    Timo says he has trouble with water, then looks relieved when you ask what he means. Dr Daniyal appears as a fictional teaching character.

    Patient

    Ich habe Probleme mit dem Wasserlassen.

    English meaning

    I have problems passing urine.

    Dr Daniyal

    Brennt es beim Wasserlassen?

    English meaning

    Does it burn when you pass urine?

    Patient

    Ja, dabei brennt es.

    English meaning

    Yes, it burns when I do that.

    Dr Daniyal

    Müssen Sie auch häufiger als sonst zur Toilette?

    English meaning

    Do you also need to go to the toilet more often than usual?

    Practice misstep

    The conversation gets stuck

    Practice misstep: Urinary symptoms: burning, frequency, urgency and low volume all present.

    Better approach

    Make room for understanding

    Ask one concrete question at a time and distinguish burning from frequency and volume. Record only confirmed answers, leaving unanswered parts of the symptom history open.

    What changed—and why it helps¶

    One vague phrase can contain several different experiences. Separating them makes the conversation easier and prevents a broad label from growing into a list of invented positives.
