    A scan is a question, not a verdict in advance

    Nele hears the word “compression” and pictures someone squeezing her painful leg as a test of bravery. She asks whether the scan has already proved there is a clot. The appointment letter contains several technical terms and exactly zero helpful sketches. The learner needs to explain the purpose of the proposed ultrasound, invite her to mention discomfort, and avoid promising that it will be completely painless or settle every possible cause in one step. Nele wants a realistic picture of what is being proposed before agreeing.

    Nele Graf, 32

    German words · human meaning

    Pull up a chair. Listen first.

    Explaining an ultrasound proposed for suspected venous thrombosis. Dr Palwasha appears as a fictional teaching character.

    Patient

    Wird mein Bein dabei stark gedrückt?

    English meaning

    Will my leg be pressed hard during it?

    Dr Palwasha

    Bei der Untersuchung werden die Venen mit Ultraschall beurteilt; dabei kann Druck mit dem Schallkopf nötig sein.

    English meaning

    The veins are assessed with ultrasound; pressure with the probe may be needed.

    Patient

    Wenn es weh tut, kann ich das sagen?

    English meaning

    If it hurts, can I say so?

    Dr Palwasha

    Ja, bitte. Sagen Sie jederzeit, wenn etwas unangenehm ist.

    English meaning

    Yes, please. Say at any time if something is uncomfortable.

    Practice misstep

    The conversation gets stuck

    Practice misstep: It is painless, completely risk-free and will answer everything.

    Better approach

    Make room for understanding

    Describe the proposed scan in plain language and explain that it helps assess the veins. Ask what Nele is worried about and invite questions before the procedure. Avoid absolute comfort or result guarantees. Make clear that the clinical team interprets the findings alongside the history and decides whether further assessment is needed.

    What changed—and why it helps¶

    Useful consent rests on an understandable expectation, not a comforting slogan. A patient can agree while still knowing that discomfort is possible and results need interpretation. Inviting feedback during the procedure gives Nele an active role without promising that any particular finding will emerge.

    Best next move: Purpose, process and invitation to speak up

    Purpose, process and a chance to ask questions create a realistic explanation. A guarantee of no discomfort is not justified, and a diagnosis before results confuses the reason for the test with its outcome. The learner should keep the proposed examination distinct from the conclusion that may follow.
